Programming How a Game Moves and Responds

In MS Pain, players switch between a character and its shadow using a limited supply of swap charges. My contributions included swap items, enemy patrol work, moving platforms and testing shortcuts.

This page shows two examples: platforms that move on a repeatable cycle, and an item that restores a charge only when the player touches it.

Brian Tam · C# gameplay programmer on a university team project

1. Making platforms move predictably

Moving platforms give a player a timing challenge. I added vertical platform movement; this example shows platforms reversing at their endpoints and starting at different points in the cycle.

2. Making a pickup follow the game rules

A swap charge lets the player switch characters in the game. A pickup restores one charge, but only on contact and only up to four. This example isolates those rules so they are easy to see.

Start the example to see a charge used and collected. No controls needed.

Or try it yourself: move the blue player onto the gold pickup, use a charge, then collect it again.

Swap charges: 2 / 4

Move right to reach the pickup.

Keyboard: A/D or arrow keys move; E collects; C refills. At 4/4 the supply is full: use a charge before collecting again. The refill shortcut makes repeated testing faster.

About this demonstration: This is a standalone browser adaptation using simple shapes; it demonstrates movement and charge rules, not character switching or the full game. Implementation references: VerticalMovingPlatform.cs, ExtraSwapItem.cs and CheatCode.cs. The original scripts run in PainterEngine; this page adapts their movement and resource rules to JavaScript.
